1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This System Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the functional and non-functional requirements for a custom Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system. The central purpose of the system is to capture, manage, and track sales inquiries that originate from Meta advertising campaigns (Facebook and Instagram) and arrive through the organisation’s WhatsApp Business account, and to manage those inquiries through their full lifecycle as leads.
The document is written for the in-house development team and is intended to be the single, authoritative reference from which the system is designed, built, tested, and accepted. Where design choices materially affect the build, the document states a recommended approach together with the reasoning behind it.
1.2 Scope
The CRM will be built in-house as a custom application. Its primary responsibility is the end-to-end management of leads generated by Meta campaigns via WhatsApp, from the moment an inquiry is received through to conversion or closure. In scope:
•	Automated capture of inbound WhatsApp inquiries that originate from Meta (Click-to-WhatsApp) campaigns, including the campaign/ad attribution data Meta attaches to the first message.
•	Recording of the customer’s reply to the automated WhatsApp greeting, and creation of a lead record from that interaction.
•	Core CRM lead-management capabilities: follow-up tracking, categorisation, manual create/edit/delete, search, assignment, and reporting.
•	Role-based access for sales agents, team leads, and administrators.
The following are out of scope for this release and noted here to prevent ambiguity:
•	Creation or management of the Meta ad campaigns themselves (handled in Meta Ads Manager).
•	The WhatsApp automatic-reply (greeting) message itself, which is configured inside the WhatsApp Business product and is treated by this system as an existing, external capability.
•	Full accounting/invoicing, marketing-email automation, and telephony, unless added in a later phase.
1.3 Intended Audience
The primary audience is the in-house development team — engineers, QA, and the technical lead — who will implement and verify the system. Secondary readers include the project sponsor and the sales team who own the business process. Sections 1 and 2 give the shared context; Sections 3 to 8 carry the detail the development team will build against.

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
The CRM is a new, self-contained web application. It sits at the centre of the lead workflow and connects outward to one key external system — WhatsApp — which is the channel through which Meta campaign inquiries arrive. The CRM does not replace WhatsApp or Meta Ads Manager; it consumes the inquiries they generate and becomes the system of record for every lead thereafter.

Context at a glance
Meta Ads Manager  →  runs Facebook/Instagram campaigns with a Click-to-WhatsApp call-to-action.
WhatsApp Business  →  receives the inquiry, sends the automatic greeting, and (via the Cloud API) forwards messages to the CRM.
CRM (this system)  →  captures the lead, stores the conversation and attribution, and drives follow-up, categorisation and reporting.
2.2 Product Functions (Summary)
At a high level the system will:
•	Receive inbound WhatsApp messages that originate from Meta campaigns and automatically create or update a lead.
•	Capture and store the campaign/ad attribution that Meta attaches to the first message, so leads can be traced back to the campaign that produced them.
•	Record the customer’s reply to the automatic greeting and store the full message thread against the lead.
•	Let agents log and schedule follow-ups, and remind them when follow-ups are due.
•	Let users categorise, tag, assign, search, filter, and manually add/edit/delete leads.
•	Provide dashboards and reports on lead volume, source, status, and agent performance.
2.3 User Classes and Characteristics
User class	Description & key needs	Technical skill
Sales Agent	Works leads day to day: reads inquiries, replies, logs follow-ups, updates status and category.	Low–Medium
Team Lead / Manager	Assigns and reassigns leads, monitors the pipeline and agent performance, runs reports.	Medium
Administrator	Manages users, roles, categories, WhatsApp/webhook configuration, and system settings.	Medium–High
System (automated)	The webhook/integration process that ingests WhatsApp messages and creates leads without human action.	n/a
2.4 Operating Environment
The system is a browser-based web application, expected to run on modern desktop and mobile browsers. It exposes a secure, publicly reachable HTTPS endpoint to receive WhatsApp webhooks. Specific language, framework, database, and hosting choices are left to the development team; the requirements in this document are deliberately technology-neutral. Any stack chosen must be able to serve HTTPS with a valid TLS certificate, verify webhook signatures, and process inbound events asynchronously (see Section 6).
2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints
•	The WhatsApp channel is governed by Meta’s WhatsApp Business Platform policies, including the 24-hour customer-service window and template-message rules for messaging outside that window.
•	The webhook endpoint must acknowledge Meta’s POST requests quickly (within Meta’s timeout, commonly cited as ~20 seconds) and therefore must process messages asynchronously.
•	Inbound event delivery is at-least-once: the system must be idempotent and de-duplicate repeated webhook deliveries by message ID.
•	Personal contact data (names, phone numbers, message content) is stored, so the system must follow applicable data-protection obligations (see Section 7.5).
2.6 Assumptions and Dependencies
•	The organisation has, or will create, a WhatsApp Business account and a verified Meta Business account.
•	The automatic greeting reply already exists in the WhatsApp Business product and does not need to be built by this system.
•	The organisation is willing to adopt the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API), directly or through a BSP, to enable automated lead capture (see Section 6 for why this is required).
•	A public HTTPS endpoint and a domain/TLS certificate will be available to host the webhook receiver.
 
3. Meta Campaign Lead Management (Core Focus)
This section describes the primary business process the CRM must support end to end. It is the focus of the project. The process moves an inquiry from a Meta ad, through WhatsApp, into the CRM as a managed lead.
3.1 End-to-End Process Overview
The lifecycle has five stages. The first two occur outside the CRM (in Meta and WhatsApp); the CRM owns everything from stage three onward.
Stage	What happens	Owned by
1	A prospect sees a Facebook/Instagram ad and taps its "Send message / Chat on WhatsApp" call-to-action.	Meta Ads Manager (external)
2	WhatsApp opens a chat with the business number. The prospect sends the first message; WhatsApp sends the automatic greeting reply back.	WhatsApp Business (external, existing)
3	The inbound message — carrying Meta campaign/ad attribution — is delivered to the CRM, which creates a new lead.	CRM (this system)
4	When the prospect replies to the greeting, that reply and any further messages are stored against the lead.	CRM (this system)
5	Agents work the lead: follow-ups, categorisation, assignment, status changes, through to conversion or closure.	CRM (this system)
3.2 Stage 1 — Meta Campaign (Click-to-WhatsApp)
Inquiries are directed to the WhatsApp Business account from Meta campaigns. In practice this is done with a Click-to-WhatsApp (CTWA) ad: the ad’s call-to-action opens a WhatsApp conversation with the business rather than a web form. This matters technically because CTWA is what allows Meta to attach campaign attribution to the first WhatsApp message, so the CRM can later report which campaign, ad set, and ad produced each lead. The CRM does not create these ads, but it depends on them being set up as Click-to-WhatsApp so the attribution data flows through.
3.3 Stage 2 — WhatsApp Inbound and Automatic Reply
When the prospect’s first message arrives, WhatsApp Business sends an automatic greeting reply. This auto-reply capability already exists within WhatsApp and is not built by the CRM. The first inbound message also opens the 24-hour customer-service window, during which the business (and later, the CRM, if outbound messaging is added) may send free-form replies. The CRM treats the auto-reply as a given and concerns itself with capturing the message and the customer’s subsequent response.

Important distinction
The automatic first reply is handled by WhatsApp’s own greeting-message feature. 
The customer’s response to that greeting is what the CRM must capture and turn into an actionable lead record, together with the original message and the campaign attribution.
3.4 Stage 3 & 4 — Lead Capture into the CRM
For the CRM to receive these messages automatically, the WhatsApp Business account must be connected to the CRM through the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) and a webhook. When a message is sent to the business number, Meta delivers an HTTPS POST to the CRM’s webhook endpoint. The CRM then:
1.	Verifies the request came from Meta (signature validation) and acknowledges it immediately.
2.	Reads the sender’s WhatsApp phone number and profile name from the payload.
3.	Reads the campaign attribution (the referral object) if the message came from a CTWA ad — the very first message of a conversation.
4.	Looks up whether a lead already exists for that phone number. If not, it creates one; if so, it appends the message to the existing lead (de-duplicating by message ID).
5.	Stores the message body, direction, and timestamp against the lead, so the customer’s reply to the greeting is captured as part of the thread.
6.	Sets the lead’s source to the originating campaign and marks it "New" for an agent to pick up.
3.4.1 Data captured from the WhatsApp payload
The following fields are available from the inbound webhook and should be captured. Exact field names must be confirmed against Meta’s official documentation before implementation.
Data	Source in payload	Stored as
Customer WhatsApp number	messages[].from	Lead phone number (unique key)
Customer display name	contacts[].profile.name	Lead name (editable later)
Message text	messages[].text.body	Message record on the lead thread
Message ID	messages[].id	De-duplication key
Timestamp	messages[].timestamp	Message time / last-activity time
Campaign source URL	referral.source_url	Lead attribution
Ad / post ID	referral.source_id	Lead attribution (which ad)
Source type	referral.source_type	Attribution (ad vs post)
Ad headline & body	referral.headline / referral.body	Attribution context on the lead
Click identifier	referral.ctwa_clid	Attribution / conversion tracking

Note on attribution: the referral object is present only on the first message of a CTWA conversation. The CRM must capture it at lead creation and persist it; it will not reappear on later messages.
3.5 Process Flow (Sequence)
The following sequence summarises the automated path from ad click to a captured lead:
Step	Actor	Action
1	Prospect	Taps the Click-to-WhatsApp ad on Facebook/Instagram.
2	WhatsApp	Opens a chat; prospect sends first message; WhatsApp sends the automatic greeting.
3	Meta → CRM	Meta POSTs the inbound message (with referral attribution) to the CRM webhook over HTTPS.
4	CRM	Validates the request, acknowledges within seconds, queues the event for background processing.
5	CRM	Creates a new lead (or appends to an existing one), storing contact, message, and attribution.
6	Prospect	Replies to the greeting; the reply is delivered by the same webhook and stored on the lead thread.
7	Agent	Sees the new lead in the CRM and begins follow-up.

6. Recommended WhatsApp Integration Approach
You asked for a recommendation on how WhatsApp should connect to the CRM. This section gives that recommendation with reasoning, so the development team can plan the integration with eyes open.
6.1 The key constraint
The consumer WhatsApp Business App (the mobile app) cannot push incoming messages into another system automatically — it has no webhook or messaging API. Its automatic greeting works, but there is no supported way for it to hand each incoming message to the CRM. To capture leads automatically, the business number must be connected through the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API), which is Meta’s API product and the only route that provides the webhook the CRM needs.
6.2 Recommendation
Recommended approach
Adopt the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API), integrated directly with Meta.
This gives the CRM a real-time webhook for inbound messages, delivers the Click-to-WhatsApp referral/attribution data needed to trace leads back to campaigns, and — because it is hosted by Meta — removes the need to run WhatsApp messaging infrastructure yourselves. It is the most direct and lowest-cost-per-message option for an in-house team comfortable with API work.
If the team prefers not to manage low-level API onboarding (business verification, template approval, phone-number registration, message queuing), a Meta Business Solution Provider (BSP) such as 360dialog, Twilio, or WATI can sit in front of the Cloud API and provide a friendlier interface, at a per-message or subscription premium. The CRM design does not change; only the integration layer does. For a custom in-house build the direct Cloud API is recommended as the primary path, with a BSP as a fallback if onboarding effort becomes a bottleneck.
6.3 Options compared
Option	Automated capture?	Notes
WhatsApp Business App (mobile)	No	Greeting auto-reply works, but no API/webhook — cannot feed the CRM. Not viable for this requirement.
Cloud API — direct (recommended)	Yes	Real-time webhook, referral attribution, Meta-hosted. Requires API development and Meta onboarding.
Cloud API via BSP	Yes	Same capability with easier onboarding and support; added cost. Good fallback.
6.4 What the integration requires
•	Accounts & IDs: a verified Meta Business account, a WhatsApp Business Account (WABA), a registered phone number with its Phone Number ID, and a permanent access token (system-user token).
•	Webhook endpoint: a public HTTPS URL with a valid TLS certificate, a verify token for the initial handshake, and signature validation (X-Hub-Signature-256) on every request.
•	Reliability: acknowledge fast (within Meta’s ~20-second limit), process asynchronously via a queue, and de-duplicate by message ID (delivery is at-least-once).
•	Messaging rules: free-form replies are allowed only inside the 24-hour customer-service window; outside it, pre-approved template messages are required. Relevant if outbound messaging is added later.
•	Cost model: the platform uses conversation-based pricing; the team should review current rates as part of planning.
6.5 Phased delivery (suggested)
Phase	Focus
Phase 1	Cloud API onboarding + webhook receiver; automated lead capture with attribution; basic lead list and detail.
Phase 2	Follow-ups, categorisation, assignment, search/filter, notifications, manual add/edit/delete.
Phase 3	Dashboards and reporting, exports, campaign attribution reporting; optional two-way messaging from the CRM.
 
7. Non-Functional Requirements
7.1 Performance
•	The webhook endpoint shall acknowledge Meta’s requests within the platform’s time limit (~20 seconds) and shall be designed to respond in under a few seconds under normal load.
•	The lead list and lead detail views shall load within 2–3 seconds under expected data volumes.
•	The system shall handle bursts of inbound messages during active campaigns without message loss, using asynchronous processing.
7.2 Reliability & Availability
•	No inbound WhatsApp message shall be lost: events shall be queued and retried on transient failure.
•	The webhook receiver should target high availability, as downtime risks missing incoming leads.
•	The system shall be idempotent so retried webhook deliveries never create duplicate leads or messages.
7.3 Scalability
•	The system shall scale to growing lead volumes and additional agents without redesign.
•	The data model shall support at least tens of thousands of leads and their full message history.
7.4 Security
•	All traffic shall be over HTTPS/TLS; the webhook shall validate Meta’s request signature.
•	Access tokens and secrets shall be stored securely (not in source control) and be rotatable.
•	Access shall be authenticated and authorised by role; users see only what their role permits.
•	Significant actions shall be captured in an audit log.
7.5 Data Protection & Compliance
•	The system stores personal data (names, phone numbers, message content) and shall handle it in line with applicable data-protection law and WhatsApp Business policies.
•	A data-retention policy shall govern how long leads and messages are kept, with a mechanism to delete or anonymise on request.
•	Access to customer message content shall be limited to authorised users.
7.6 Usability
•	The interface shall be clear enough for low-to-medium-skill sales agents with minimal training.
•	The system shall be usable on both desktop and mobile browsers.
•	Common actions (log follow-up, change status, categorise) shall be reachable in as few steps as possible.
7.7 Maintainability
•	The integration layer shall be isolated so a change from direct Cloud API to a BSP (or vice versa) does not require rewriting the CRM core.
•	Configuration (tokens, category lists, assignment rules) shall be manageable without code changes where practical.

8.1 Key Risks & Mitigations

The technical document (SRS) specifically states that the technology choices are "technology-neutral"—meaning it leaves the exact coding languages up to your development team, as long as the system meets the business rules (like handling webhooks in under 20 seconds).
However, since you are a web development agency building this for a tourism business, here is the highly recommended, modern tech stack to build this CRM quickly, securely, and reliably.
You can share this with your lead developer:
1. Frontend (What the Agents & Managers See)
Framework: React.js (with Next.js) or Vue.js.
Styling: Tailwind CSS.
Why: These are the industry standards. They are incredibly fast, work perfectly on both desktop and mobile browsers, and are great for building complex dashboards and chat interfaces.
2. Backend (The Brain & Webhook Receiver)
Language/Framework: Node.js (using Express or NestJS) OR PHP (using Laravel).
Why Node.js? It is exceptionally fast at handling multiple webhooks at the exact same time (which is exactly what Meta will send you).
Why Laravel? If your agency already uses PHP, Laravel is the fastest way to build a full CRM with user roles, databases, and dashboards out-of-the-box.
3. Database (Where the Data Lives)
Database: PostgreSQL.
Why: It is a relational database, which is perfect for a CRM. It easily connects a "Lead" to their "Messages," "Follow-ups," and "Campaign Data." It also handles JSON data beautifully (which is how Meta sends the webhook data).
4. The "Secret Sauce" (Crucial for Meta Webhooks)
Message Queue: Redis (with BullMQ if using Node.js).
Why this is mandatory: The SRS document states your system must reply to Meta in under 20 seconds. If 50 tourists message you at the exact same second, your database might be too slow to save them all instantly. Redis acts as a "waiting room." It instantly tells Meta "Message received!" in 1 second, and then processes the data in the background safely without crashing your system.
5. Real-Time Features (For the Chat Screen)
Technology: Socket.io or Pusher.
Why: When a tourist replies on WhatsApp, the agent's screen in the CRM should update instantly like a real chat app, without the agent having to refresh the page.
6. Hosting & Infrastructure
Cloud Provider: AWS (Amazon Web Services) or DigitalOcean.
Why: You need a secure, HTTPS-enabled server with a valid TLS certificate (as required by Meta). DigitalOcean is very easy for agencies to set up and manage, while AWS is better if you expect massive scale.
